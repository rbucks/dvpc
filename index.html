§ 01 — About

One district. One application. Every kind of business.

This is a 4CD competition. If you are enrolled in a Contra Costa Community College District course — any course, at any of our colleges — you are eligible to apply.

For the past several years, the Contra Costa Community College District has hosted an annual Business Pitch Competition at DVC's Pleasant Hill campus — student founders sharing ventures spanning wellness tech, food sustainability, hardware, and student support. Past winners have gone on to pilot with Contra Costa County agencies and BART.

For 2026 we've widened the door without leaving the district. Community members are welcome to compete — they just have to be enrolled in a 4CD class. That includes non-credit Community Education courses: a ceramics class, a QuickBooks workshop, an ESL course, a welding certificate. One class is enough. Enroll, then apply.

There are no tracks and no categories. Students, dual-enrolled high schoolers, and community members in a non-credit class all fill out the same form and stand on the same stage. What we are actively recruiting is range — see the room we're trying to build.

§ 02 — The room

We want a room that looks like the Diablo Valley.

An hour northeast of Silicon Valley.
Nothing like it — on purpose.
Concord · Pleasant Hill · Martinez · Walnut Creek · Pittsburg · Antioch · Brentwood · Oakley · Clayton · Lafayette · Danville · San Ramon · Moraga · Orinda · Richmond · San Pablo · Pinole

We are not auditioning to be the other valley. We are cafés, breweries, bakeries, and nail salons. We are aviation and industry out past the airfield. We are biotech and health tech in the labs. And we are construction trades — a lot of construction trades.

That's the economy people here actually work in, so that's the room we want on this stage. There is no venture-scale requirement and no technical co-founder required. A framing contractor with a hiring plan is a business. A second bakery location is a business. Bring the software too — just don't assume it has a head start. It doesn't.

§ 06 — Scoring

Fifty points to make the cut. Fifty more on the stage.

Scoring happens in two rounds, on two published rubrics. The first is for everybody: every application submitted by 11:59pm PT on Nov 2 is scored on the screening rubric, and the ten highest-scoring teams become the finalists. The second rubric is used only at the live final, and only those ten teams are scored on it. Each round is worth 50 points, and every category is worth 10.

The two scores are completely separate — your screening score does not carry into the final. Screening decides who gets on stage and nothing more. Squeaking into the ten costs you nothing there, and topping the screening round buys you nothing either: all ten finalists start the final at zero.

Entry rules
  • Enrollment Current enrollment at Contra Costa College, Diablo Valley College, or Los Medanos College.
  • Deadline Applications close at 11:59pm PT on Monday, Nov 2. Late entries are not accepted.
  • What you submit A short description of the business idea, plus a slide presentation of no more than 15 slides. Start from the pitch deck template.
  • Team size No more than five people on a team. Solo founders are permitted.
  • Existing businesses Eligible if the business has less than $25,000 in total assets or annual revenue.
  • Competed before? Welcome back — but your new submission must be a different concept.
  • Multiple entries Allowed. A finalist still pitches only once at the live event, solo or on a team.
  • Finalists The top 10 are announced on Friday, Nov 6.
  • Where to apply One form for everybody — start your application.

§ 10 — FAQ

Common questions.

What does 4CD stand for?

The Contra Costa Community College District. It's made up of three colleges — Contra Costa College in San Pablo, Diablo Valley College in Pleasant Hill (plus its San Ramon campus), and Los Medanos College in Pittsburg (plus its Brentwood center) — along with districtwide Community Education. Enrollment at any of them, in any course, makes you eligible to compete.

Who can apply?

Anyone currently enrolled in a Contra Costa Community College District course. That's the only eligibility bar: one active enrollment at DVC, Contra Costa College, or Los Medanos College. Credit students, dual-enrolled high school students, and community members in a non-credit class all use the same application — there are no separate tracks. There are no citizenship or status requirements.

How do I apply?

Through one form: the 2026 application. It asks for a short description of your business idea and a slide presentation of no more than 15 slides — nothing else. Everyone uses the same form, and it closes at 11:59pm PT on Monday, Nov 2.

I'm not a student. Can I still compete?

Yes — enroll in a class and you're eligible. It does not have to be a business class, a full course load, or a credit course. A single non-credit Community Education class counts: cooking, ceramics, computer basics, ESL, QuickBooks, welding, yoga. Register for one 4CD course, keep the enrollment active through Nov 20, and you can apply.

Does it count if the class is non-credit Community Education?

It does. Non-credit Community Education enrollment carries exactly the same eligibility as a credit course — a single Community Education class puts you on the same footing as a full-time credit student, with no separate track and no extra paperwork.

My business isn't tech. Is it still a fit?

It's more than a fit — it's the point. We are actively recruiting Main Street: restaurants, food trucks, salons, landscaping and trades, childcare, retail, cleaning, bookkeeping, repair shops. Brick-and-mortar and service businesses compete on the same stage, in front of the same judges, against the same five scoring criteria as the software and AI teams. You do not need a technical co-founder, a prototype, or a growth chart.

Does my idea need to be a real business already?

No — a concept on a single slide is welcome. You may also pitch a business that already exists, as long as it has less than $25,000 in total assets or annual revenue. Past that, it has outgrown this competition. The screening rubric scores the description of the idea and the rationale behind it, so an early-stage team is not penalized for having no traction yet — but supporting evidence, where you have it, is worth points.

Can I apply with a co-founder or team?

Yes. A team may have no more than five people, and solo founders are absolutely permitted. List every team member on the application; only listed members are eligible to present on stage.

What does the application include?

Two things: a short description of the business idea, and a slide presentation of no more than 15 slides. No video at this stage. Everything is due by 11:59pm PT on Monday, Nov 2. Finalists then submit a final-draft deck and an optional 1-minute YouTube video. Open the application form →

Is there a pitch deck template?

Yes — the 10-slide pitch deck template, and it's the best place to start if you've never built a deck. After a title slide, there's one slide each for the problem, your solution, your team, the customer, how you sell to them, how many customers there are, your traction so far, how you'll scale your marketing, and your ask. That leaves room under the 15-slide limit for anything specific to your business. The template is view-only: use File → Make a copy to get your own version in Google Slides, or File → Download → Microsoft PowerPoint to work in PowerPoint. Using it is optional.

Can I submit more than one application?

Yes, you can submit multiple applications. But a finalist pitches only once at the live event, whether solo or as part of a team — so if more than one of your entries makes the top ten, you will be asked to choose which one goes on stage.

I entered a previous year. Can I compete again?

Yes — past participants are welcome back, including past winners. The one condition is that your new submission must be a different concept from the one you pitched before. Come back with something new.

What kind of mentoring do finalists get?

The mentoring window is a full two weeks — it opens when finalists are named on Friday, Nov 6 and runs to the final on Friday, Nov 20. Every finalist team is matched with one or more mentors drawn from 4CD business faculty, alumni founders, past judges, and working local operators. Support includes deck reviews, pitch coaching, financial-model feedback, and live dry-runs in the actual venue. Main Street teams are matched with people who have run storefronts and payrolls, not only startup advisors.

Is there a weekly Zoom session I can join?

Yes — a live Zoom drop-in every Wednesday at 4pm PT, running weekly through the competition. Bring your deck or your questions; it's the same kind of help you'd get in the Discord, just live and face-to-face. Join the Wednesday Zoom → (Meeting ID 819 5141 5734, passcode vikings).

Do I keep ownership of my idea?

Yes. You retain 100% ownership of your venture, IP, and equity. The competition takes no equity, no fees, and no rights to your work. You grant us the right to use your name, logo, and pitch summary for promotional purposes only.

Is the final event open to the public?

Yes — and we encourage it. The November 20 final is free to attend, and attendance requires no enrollment of any kind. Invite your family, your customers, your potential investors, and your skeptics. The audience vote is real.

Is there a flyer I can print about this competition?

Yes — two, both sized for US Letter and free to post anywhere on campus or around town. Print at 100% / actual size; don't let the print dialog scale to fit.
